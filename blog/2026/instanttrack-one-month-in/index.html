InstantTrack, one month in: 35 repos and 4 papers

Created on November 26, 2026

2026   ·   instanttrack   motion-tracking   open-source   ·   projects

On 29 October I released InstantTrack: one whole-body motion-tracking policy for all 40 LAFAN1 clips (2.45 h of motion) on the Unitree G1, trained on one RTX 5090 in 15 minutes. Four weeks later it trains in under 3 minutes, and other people have built on it in ways I did not plan for. This post records both.

From 15 minutes to under 3

Every release is measured the same way: train until the deployed policy reaches a normalized return of 0.82 with 0 of 40 clips failed, and holds it for three checkpoints in a row. The baseline is SuperMimic with a policy EMA on the same machine, simulator, MDP and evaluator, which takes 0.55 h.

v1.3 4 Nov ≤ 3 min ≥ 10.9× BlinkMimic’s changes merged back: structured world model, mirror augmentation, evaluation-guided resets, overlapped CUDA streams
v1.4 7 Nov 2.74 min   Overlapped critic update, 11.87 it/s (from 10.28)
v1.4.1 23 Nov     CITATION.cff and a Zenodo DOI

From v1.3 on, checkpoints are evaluated every 100 iterations instead of every 1.5 to 3 minutes, so the times are finer than the old schedule could show. The v1.4 figure is the mean of 15 seeds (range 2.21–3.61 min). The changelog has every run, including the changes that did not help.

A fork that came back

The biggest single step, v1.2 to v1.3, did not come from me. Fuyi Chen forked InstantTrack v1.2, made it faster and released it as BlinkMimic. Because both projects are MIT-licensed, I could merge those changes back into InstantTrack v1.3 with credit, and then build v1.4 on top. BlinkMimic was in turn rebuilt in JAX as XMimic, which trains the same task in under a minute on four H100s.

That loop is the main reason I keep the code open and every run logged: someone else can pick up a result, check it, and push it further than I would alone.
